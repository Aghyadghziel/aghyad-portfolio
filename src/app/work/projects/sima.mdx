## The brief

SIMA Studio is my own studio. It designs and builds websites for Saudi companies and brands — Arabic first, with 3D and motion where they earn their place.

The site had to do three jobs at once: show the work, explain what a project costs and how long it takes before anyone has to ask, and turn a visitor into a conversation. It also had to be the studio's own proof: if the work is meant to be premium, the studio's website cannot be the weakest thing in the portfolio.

## What I built

**The identity.** Ink, bone and ash with one saffron accent, a two-part S symbol, Barlow Condensed for display and Alexandria for Arabic. The same system runs from the brand deck through to the favicon.

**Arabic as the default.** The apex domain lands on `/ar`; English mirrors it. Every line of copy is written in both languages rather than machine-translated, with type chosen separately for each script and the whole layout designed right to left first.

**Case studies, not thumbnails.** Each project gets its own page: the brief, the constraint, the idea, the interactions, the stack. Content lives in typed TypeScript files, so a project is data rather than markup, and the index, the case study and the metadata all read from the same source.

**Pricing in the open.** Packages, what is and is not included, timelines and the payment split are on the page. That removes the first email exchange from every lead.

**Contact and leads.** The contact form validates on the server, writes the lead to a Postgres table on Neon and sends the notification through Resend, so nothing depends on an inbox being watched.

**Analytics I own.** First-party and cookieless: page views, journeys and active sessions stored in my own database and read back in an admin dashboard behind an email-code login, with my own devices excluded so my testing does not pollute the numbers.

**The hero.** A spectral light arc rendered in React Three Fiber, kept deliberately light — the 3D is atmosphere, not the pitch.
